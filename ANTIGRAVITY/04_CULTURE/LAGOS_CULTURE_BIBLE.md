# DOCUMENT 04 — LAGOS CULTURE BIBLE
## Authenticity, Sociocultural Systems & Urban Soul

> **The Authenticity Rule**: Culture in Project Eko is a living, functional system, not a decorative skin or caricature. A Nigerian player must recognize the psychological rhythm, sensory atmosphere, street vocabulary, and communal hustle of Lagos before they even register the 3D polygon.

## 2. Sensory Urban Atmosphere & Soundscapes

Lagos sounds like nowhere else on Earth. The game audio system uses a multi-channel spatial sound matrix layered by district, time of day, and weather:

### 2.1 The Daily Audio Rhythm
* **05:00 – 07:00 (The Dawn Awakening)**:
  * Early call to prayer echoing from neighborhood mosques across Mushin and Lagos Island.
  * Church choirs and early morning prayer bells in Surulere.
  * Roosters crowing behind residential compounds; metal gates clattering open.
  * Bus conductors clearing their throats and slamming the side panels of yellow Danfos (*"CMS straight! Yaba entering!"*).
* **08:00 – 17:00 (The Workday & Commute Rush)**:
  * Cacophony of dual-tone vehicle horns in stationary traffic (*go-slow*).
  * Roar of commercial generators humming behind corporate towers in Victoria Island.
  * Market traders drumming on wooden counters, clapping hands, and singing prices at Tejuosho and Balogun.
  * Traffic radio broadcasters giving live updates on Third Mainland Bridge congestion.
* **18:00 – 22:00 (The Evening Bustle)**:
  * Sizzling sound of street suya on glowing charcoal grills; street vendors shouting *"Cold pure water! Soft drinks!"* through car windows.
  * Open-air barbershops blasting Afrobeats and street-pop hits through outdoor stereo speakers.
* **23:00 – 04:00 (Nightlife & High-Life)**:
  * Bass-heavy sound systems pumping from clubs in VI and Lekki Phase 1.
  * Private generator sets thrumming in gated estates; distant security whistles from neighborhood guards.

## 3. Core Societal & Cultural Systems

### 3.1 The "Generator / NEPA" Power System
In Lagos, electrical power is not an invisible, passive utility—it is an active operational calculation:
* **The Grid (NEPA/PHCN)**: Unpredictable availability. When the grid fails, home and shop lights go out, factory machinery stops, and computers shut down.
* **The Generator ("I Better Pass My Neighbour" to Heavy Industrial Diesels)**:
  * Small businesses and apartments rely on generators that consume petrol or diesel.
  * Players must budget Game Naira for daily fuel. If your Computer Village tech repair shop runs out of fuel while fixing a client's laptop, you risk burning the motherboard or missing the delivery deadline!
  * Owning an inverter/solar setup in your Ikoyi or Lekki home is a major mid-game status symbol that eliminates fuel management headaches.

### 3.2 The Street Economy & Roadside Commerce
Lagos traffic jams (*go-slow*) are not dead time; they are living, drive-through retail markets!
* While stuck on Carter Bridge or Oshodi-Apapa Expressway, street hawkers approach vehicle windows selling:
  * Plantain chips, gala sausage rolls, roasted groundnuts, cold bottled water, peeled oranges.
  * Phone chargers, windscreen wipers, steering wheel covers, world maps, newspapers.
* Players driving cars can roll down their windows to buy food (restoring stamina/hunger) or vehicle accessories on the fly.

### 3.3 The Social Contract of the "Area Boys" & Agberos
Urban street transport unions and local youth groups are an authentic fixture of Lagos street life:
* At bus stops and intersections, union officials (*Agberos*) with distinctive shirts collect daily park tickets and loading dues from commercial Danfo and Keke drivers.
* Failing to pay dues can cause heated dialogue, cracked side mirrors, or delays in passenger loading.
* Players building high street reputation (*Respect / Omo Onile status*) can negotiate bulk daily permits or unlock safe passage through tricky transit corridors.

### 3.4 Owambe & Weekend Party Culture
Saturday in Lagos is dedicated to celebrations:
* **Aso-Ebi (Uniform Ceremonial Attire)**: Players purchase coordinated luxury lace, brocade, and agbada fabrics from Balogun Market tailors to attend prestigious weddings, chieftaincy celebrations, and milestone birthdays.
* **Spraying Culture**: At owambe party venues, guests celebrate musicians and hosts through celebratory token spraying, boosting social prestige, charisma stats, and unlocking high-value business partnerships.
* **Jollof Rice Hierarchy**: Authentic party food served at events; authentic "Party Jollof" cooked over firewood grants unique energy and productivity buffs!

### 3.5 Faith & Community Institutions
* Churches and Mosques are architectural and community anchors across Lagos.
* They serve as peaceful sanctuaries, social safety nets, and community gathering spots.
* **Ethical Boundary**: In accordance with the project's regulatory and moral guardrails, faith institutions are represented with dignity and respect, and are **never** associated with monetized wagering or commercial gambling mechanics.
